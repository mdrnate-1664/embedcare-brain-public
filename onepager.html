The EmbedCare Brain

One-page summary, 24 October 2026

EmbedCare is a white-label telehealth platform. In about six weeks its president, Nate, built a system where every conversation the company has lands in one private, versioned store. Scheduled AI routines read that store and do the day's operating work: they file records, draft tickets and replies, bill signed contracts, keep the CRM current, prepare the daily meeting and watch for errors and privacy leaks. The few decisions that need a person reach Nate's phone with Send, Hold and Skip buttons.

Four layers

1
Capture

Email, meeting notes, call transcripts, Telegram, accounting reports, portal events and bookings land in the store automatically. Duplicates are removed, credentials redacted, and every change is scanned for secrets.

2
Index and records

A build job turns the raw files into an index, a list of every promise anyone made with its due date, and one record per client.

3
Operate

About forty scheduled routines and jobs on a day clock from 06:00 to 19:30. Each has one job and leaves a file the next one reads.

4
Guardrails

Output checks with a deadline per job, an error watch, a daily privacy scan, and nightly snapshots so any change can be rolled back.

One ordinary weekday

06:00Call transcripts and the chat digest filed
06:15 to 06:50Email filed, index rebuilt, promises nudged
07:15 to 07:20Meeting notes to the CRM, promises extracted
07:30 to 07:45Ticket drafts, privacy scan, error check
08:00A signed contract is billed, untouched by hand
08:30 to 08:53Agenda built, pitches answered in draft
09:00Five drafts reach Nate's phone
14:00 to 14:20Accounting reports, overdue invoices
16:00 to 18:00Contracts again, tickets, an account update
19:30New leads become CRM deals with owners
Nothing reaches a client without a human tap.

Filing, indexing, CRM upkeep and invoice instructions to the company's own accounting team run on their own. Every message to a client or partner stays a draft until Nate taps Send.

What it is not yet

Capture
9 of 10

Every channel lands in one versioned store, deduplicated, redacted and scanned.

Operate
7 of 10

Real work ships daily, but every approval goes through one person and nothing measures cycle times yet.

Serve
4 of 10

It answers Nate, not yet the team or clients. Client questions are still answered by hand.

Being built next, in order

  1. Client records, finished. Routines write to one record per client instead of re-deriving it.
  2. Status answers for the team. "Where is this client" answered from stored answers, approved by an account manager.
  3. The metrics layer. Reply times, signed to live and overdue promises per owner, computed so they trend.
